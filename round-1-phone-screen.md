# Round 1: Phone Screen / Initial Technical Screen

## **Overview**
- **Duration**: 45-60 minutes
- **Format**: Phone call or video call
- **Goal**: Filter for communication skills, experience level, and basic technical competence
- **Interviewer**: Usually a recruiter or junior engineer

---

## **Common Topics**

### **1. Background & Experience**
- Walk me through your resume
- Tell me about your current/recent role
- What are your main responsibilities?
- How long have you been working with Java/Angular/PostgreSQL?
- Why are you looking for a new role?

**Tips:**
- Keep it to 2-3 minutes
- Focus on full-stack contributions, not just backend or frontend
- Highlight projects that used all three technologies

### **2. Technical Stack Overview**

**Java Questions:**
- What version of Java are you most comfortable with?
- Spring vs Spring Boot - what's the difference?
- What's your experience with microservices?
- Have you worked with Maven/Gradle?
- JPA/Hibernate experience?

**Angular Questions:**
- Which versions of Angular have you worked with?
- TypeScript experience - comfort level?
- RxJS observables - how comfortable are you?
- Angular best practices you follow?
- State management (NgRx, Akita, etc.)?

**PostgreSQL Questions:**
- How comfortable are you writing complex queries?
- Have you optimized slow queries?
- Indexing strategies?
- Data modeling and normalization?
- Connection pooling experience?

### **3. Full-Stack Integration**
- How have you connected your Angular frontend to Java backend?
- REST APIs - design experience?
- Authentication/Authorization setup? (JWT, OAuth, etc.)
- API versioning strategies?
- What tools do you use for API testing? (Postman, REST Client, etc.)

### **4. Development Practices**
- Version control - Git workflow?
- CI/CD experience?
- Testing - unit tests, integration tests?
- Code review process?
- Debugging tools you use?

### **5. Project Examples**
- "Tell me about a full-stack project you built"
  - What was the challenge?
  - How did you approach it?
  - What did you learn?

- "Describe a time you had to troubleshoot a complex issue"
  - Was it frontend, backend, or database?
  - How did you debug it?

### **6. Soft Skills / Work Style**
- How do you prefer to learn new technologies?
- Experience with Agile/Scrum?
- How do you handle tight deadlines?
- Tell me about a time you worked with a difficult team member
- Your ideal work environment?

### **7. Questions to Ask**
- What's the tech stack your team uses currently?
- What does a typical day/sprint look like?
- What's the team size?
- Growth opportunities?
- What are the biggest technical challenges the team is facing?

---

## **Common Mistakes to Avoid**

❌ Being vague about your experience
❌ Overstating your knowledge (you'll be tested deeper later)
❌ Not asking clarifying questions
❌ Complaining about previous employers
❌ Not knowing the basics of your tech stack
❌ Long-winded answers (keep to 1-2 minutes each)

---

## **What They're Evaluating**

✅ Can you communicate clearly?
✅ Is your experience actually relevant?
✅ Do you understand your tech stack?
✅ Are you excited about this role?
✅ Red flags in your background?
✅ Cultural fit signals?

---

## **Sample Answers Template**

### "Walk me through your experience with Java"

*Good structure:*
1. **Years/versions**: "I've been using Java for 5 years, primarily Java 11 and Java 17"
2. **Framework**: "Most experience with Spring Boot and Spring Web MVC"
3. **Projects**: "Built REST APIs serving millions of requests, worked on microservices architecture"
4. **Depth**: "Familiar with Spring Security, Spring Data JPA, and transaction management"
5. **Recent**: "Currently working on [specific project that's relevant]"

---

## **Interview Flow Tips**

1. **Be yourself** - They're checking culture fit too
2. **Ask permission to think** - "That's a great question, let me think for a moment..."
3. **Relate back to the role** - "That's interesting - does your team work with similar challenges?"
4. **Enthusiasm matters** - Show genuine interest in their stack and problems
5. **No right answers yet** - This is exploratory, not technical evaluation
